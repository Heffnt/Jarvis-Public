The failures design came back three times rejected

Design session for deterministic checks and VQC. Written 1:47 am Eastern, October 16, 2026. One decision of yours is at the end.

What the design was for

This week several failures on the Jarvis box went unseen. They fall into three cases:

  • Usage-limit notices stored as replies. From October 9 to 11 the briefer's and observer's replies were stored as the Claude usage-limit notice, and no one was told.
  • Processes killed with no record. On October 14 the operating system killed the gate, the session host and other processes for lack of memory. A killed process cannot report its own death, so no record row exists for any of it.
  • Repeats burying everything else. One low-disk warning was written 232 times on October 15. The record's search returns only the newest 500 rows, so it buried every other failure.

The failures design answered all three with four pieces:

PieceWhat it does
One record row per open failureA failure is one row, identified by what can recover from it plus the cleaned first line of its error. A repeat raises the row's count instead of adding a row. A later success of the same thing closes the row.
The strict reply ruleA session's reply counts as done only when Claude Code reports success and its transcript holds no error notice. Anything else is a failed reply with a row.
The journal readerA small program on the box's clock reads the system journal (the operating system's own log) and turns every failure in it into a row, including processes killed for lack of memory.
MigrationMoves the existing failure rows into the new form.

What the three audits found, read together

Codex (gpt-5.6-sol) audited the design three times: 11:45 pm, about 12:20 am and 1:03 am. Each round was rejected. The agent rules say a third rejection stops the design. It gets no fourth patch and comes back to you with every finding.

PieceAcross the three rounds
One row per open failureKept every round: "The condition-row model is a sound answer to repeated failure volume … should be retained." The remaining findings are mechanics: whether a repeat updates its row, validation order, and migration.
The strict reply ruleAccepted as a rule: "Design B's strict settlement rule fits". Round 3 found the mechanics of capture and classification largely resolved.
The journal readerFailed every round on coverage, which is the decisive finding below.
Everything elseGrew each round. Each round folded in that night's rulings: the vocabulary ban, the VQC test move, and weighing risk per change. Round 3 had 14 blocking items, most outside the three pieces.

The finding that says the design does not fit its problem is about the journal reader. The design promised to report every failure the journal records. To keep from reporting one failure twice, it listed the kinds of journal message to read and left out kinds that, in the one window of history it sampled, always appeared next to another message. Codex: "it defines universal failure coverage by omitting whole message categories whenever this one window happened to contain another entry. That is sampling the symptom, not constructing a complete failure channel." Any list of message kinds can miss the next kind, so no patch to the list makes the promise true.

A second cause shows across the rounds. One design carried four pieces plus every new ruling, so each round fixed some items and exposed others. Smaller designs, each audited on its own, avoid that.
